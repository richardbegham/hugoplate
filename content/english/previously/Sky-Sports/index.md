### Sky Sports - Football

From almost his very first day as a Freelancer, Richard was lighting studio shows discussing football.
Mostly very much was the cliched _"..game of two halves...jumpers for goalposts..."_ and so on, but there was one production that took football very seriously. Very, very, seriously... And Richard lit almost every single broadcast of this for a decade :

#### Monday Night Football (MNF)

Sky Sport's Premier League Flagship production with lead pundits Gary Neville and Jamie Carragher delivering unusually long and serious pre- and post- match coverage. This had three different iterations over Richard's time, starting with the original Studio G production first lit by Malcolm Reed (lots of MiStrip, Pixellines, and a very unhelpful header). The first of these that Richard would have gone solo on was (by coincidence) the day that the original presenters (Keys & Gray) were very abruptly & publicly sacked when tapes their behavior were [leaked to the Guardian.](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2011/jan/24/andy-gray-richard-keys-sexist-comments)

MNF pioneered the use of huge interactive touch-screens for real-time analysis, combined with the incredibly detailed tracking and statistical analysis of every player in every Premier League (PL) match. They also pushed the limits of realtime cutting-edge 3D graphics with individual footage of every PL player (shot on 2 stacked sideways 4k cameras at a time when HD was still a novelty for most people). This was mixed with insightful and fluff-free passionate commentary, to deliver an unprecedented quality - and duration! - of wrap-around analysis before, during, and after just one match. Even when it's a subject you can have no interest in, you can still tell when its being done beter.

On 30th April 2012, MNF had its highest ever viewing figures - and also the the-highest for any sporting event on Sky (eventully beaten by a free-to-air match during Covid Lockdown) that was only beaten during the hgwhen four million tuned-in to watch Manchester City defeat Manchester United 1-0 on its way to its first top flight title since 1968 (this remains the highstill their highest-ever figure, and only exceeded during the heigh to coivd locakdonw)

World-leading technologies stretched the boundaries of what could be achieved in a live studio environment with the Mixed-Reality set from 2013-2017 in Studio F, powerfully led by Senior Director Duncan East and Executive Producer Scott Melvin, who listened to and then demanded - and got - the best out of every single person working on the show, every single time.

- Sky's first million-pound Set.

The opening Team walkthrough with the Presenter (Ed Chamberlain and Dave Jones) was particularly challenging technically - starting on a MCU hard up against a green wall, doing a walk all around the Green Screen area weaving in and out of life-size players, then the camera would reveal the real set outside the green area as well, and he would walk across to the main desk, going through the boundary between Real and VR - all in one shot, live. A tricky challenge with the technology of the day and a very low ceiling meaning couldn't get the lights as far away as was need to avoid inverse square law problems.

Extensive used of dead matt black paint (real) and textures (virtual) were used to conceal the join between the Real and the Virtual, and a little LED strip was also used to hide the join. Trying to keep both the green and black floors free from dust was a nightmare, despite the entire crew being badgered to wear overshoes by the Floor Manager Frankie...the breakthrough came when Richard released what was needed was not trying to make Reality impossibly perfect, but to make the Virtual a bit scruffy. Sales of blue plastic shoes plummeted.

24x Motion Analysis cameras providing 6-D positional data to 3 cameras (first production run of Sony 4300 cameras) each with Ultimatte & Viz-RT graphics to give 360-degree VR and AR virtual set, together with the ability to weave through life-size player line-ups

Major technical challenge to get this system to reliably track smoothy, requiring frequent recalibration.
In addition, the VR keying requires significant light levels, but the small size of the studio makes separation challenging, together with a low ceiling makes the inverse square law not your friend. And then the all this green light needs to be kept from the real set - and even worse, the matt black floor real floor, which was a nightmare to keep clean and perfect.

The solution to this last problem was discovered by Richard, and it was to avoid perfection - don't try and make a real floor perfect to match a virtual one, instead make the virtual one a bit messy and scuffed-up, then the transition isn't so obvious.

If getting all that kit working once wasn't hard enough, the entire production, with all its temperamental parts had to be rigged from scratch every time, in just one day. An aspect that was a constant point of disbelief by technical visitors from other television networks from around the world.
